# Flare: Brand strategy

Owner: Brand. Version 1.0, Sat 2026-10-10. Inputs: docs/product/ (BRD, PRD v1.1, personas), founder direction (Oct 10).

## Story

Every friend group has a quiet afternoon when someone thinks "we should go outside" and nobody says it, because asking feels like a risk. Flare takes the risk out. You snap where you are. Your friends get a small flame. Each private yes makes it grow. When enough say yes, it catches, and you go. If it doesn't catch, it doesn't die in public: it glows low as a smaller plan, and then it drifts away like smoke, leaving nothing behind.

The fire is the whole brand: a small flame that grows (Kindling), catches (Lit), glows low (Ember) and drifts off (Gone).

## Positioning

**For** friend groups who want to meet outside more often than they do,
**Flare is** the private yes that turns "anyone around?" into an hour outside together,
**unlike** group chats and event apps, where every answer is public and plans either happen or die,
**because** it only goes ahead when enough friends quietly say yes, and when it doesn't, it shrinks instead of failing.

## Brand promise

Seconds on the screen, an hour outside, nothing left behind.

## Personality

| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| Warm, like a campfire after sunset | Hot, loud, urgent |
| Outdoorsy, a Michigan fall afternoon: maple, birch, pine, dusk | Techy, glassy, neon, "AI magic" |
| A little playful (a flame that wobbles, smoke that drifts) | Cute at the cost of clarity |
| Quiet and discreet (nobody sees a no) | Social-feed, gamified, competitive |
| Plain-spoken, short sentences | Jargon, hype, exclamation marks |
| Inclusive by default (big type, spoken scenes, benches) | An afterthought "accessibility mode" |

## What Flare is and isn't

| Flare is | Flare isn't |
| --- | --- |
| A way to ask without asking in public | A social network, feed or profile |
| A meetup that happens only when enough say yes | An event planner with RSVPs and reminders |
| A plan that shrinks when people are tired | A fitness tracker or streak counter |
| Open-source AI on your own phone, used for a few words | An AI companion or chatbot |
| Gone when it's over | A memory archive (Sparks are opt-in, one line) |

## Voice

Rules: buttons ≤ 3 words, messages ≤ 12 words, no exclamation marks, no AI words, never guilt.

| Moment | Say | Don't say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Send | "Send it" | "Launch your Flare now!" |
| Waiting | "Need 2 more." | "Hurry, only 2 more friends needed!" |
| Lit | "It's lit. Fuego is at the pond." | "Congratulations, your event is confirmed." |
| Ember | "Smaller plan: 15 min at the bench." | "Your Flare failed. Try again?" |
| Gone, unlit | "Not this time." | "Nobody could make it." |
| Fade | "Smaller plan: the short loop." | "Someone is tired." |
| Offline | "Offline. Saved at 2:14 PM." | "Error: network unavailable" |

## Tagline system

- **Primary:** "If enough friends say yes, it's on."
- **Short:** "Snap it. Say yes. Go."
- **Promise line:** "Seconds of screen. An hour outside."

## Why this wins attention in the field

Of 813 Week 1 entries, 22% use the "AI that wants you to stop using it" line and almost all visuals are green leaves, trails or chat bubbles (docs/research/00). Flare's look is fire at dusk, its subject is people, and the AI is never the hero of the brand. That is the gap.
