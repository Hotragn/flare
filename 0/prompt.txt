Here's the idea, from first principles. People don't stay inside for lack of places to go. They stay in because asking is awkward, nobody wants to show up alone, plans die in the group chat, some friends can't use the spot, and phones die exactly where the good places are.

I want to build Flare: you snap where you are, and friends get a short invite. It only happens if enough of them privately say yes. Nobody ever sees a "no," and if it doesn't come together it shrinks instead of dying. Open-source AI runs on the phone. It should work for friends with disabilities and in places with no signal, and it should leave nothing behind. Being there together is the reward.

Less tech, more nature. The app should get out of the way fast. The place, the season, the light and the people are the point. A good session is about 5 seconds of screen and an hour outside.

We're shipping two things: the app (installable on iPhone) and a website that explains it.

My rough notes are in ../FLARE-SPEC.md. Treat them as founder input, not orders.

What I've learned about the field:
1. Gemma on Ollama is ordinary. Nearly everyone did it, so it won't set us apart.
2. On-device in the browser is the mark of the top entries (Side Quest, TrailSnack). We're doing that, plus the laptop fallback for the iPhone 13.
3. Nobody combined group coordination (quorum), privacy with a provable deletion audit, accessibility (talking compass, needs profile), and both a Telegram bot and links for iMessage/WhatsApp. That combination is our edge, not the model choice.
4. Fully open-source data storage (Postgres) isn't something any notable entry advertised, so it's a small extra point for the "why open" section.
5. Render is crowded as a category, and Gemma is very crowded. Our real target is the Overall prize.
